Why the numbers hold

Long pauses between beats are detected – and reported separately.

Heart rate variability is associated with the activity of the autonomic nervous system — the interplay of sympathetic and parasympathetic tone. It is therefore not a readout of a state: there is no robust normal value in the horse. That is why the deviation levels rest on the horse's own history.

In a resting recording — 642 beats, 0.31 % artefact rate, rated excellent by the quality indicator — two beats, a long pause with its short follow-on beat, are enough to lift RMSSD from 24.2 to 55.5 ms, more than double – and the quality indicator never warns. A high share of plausible beats says nothing about whether the metric is right.

The method in detail: filter chain and limits

A resting horse often shows long pauses between two beats — frequently a second-degree AV block; only an ECG shows what it is. Count them in raw and SDNN and RMSSD rise dramatically, and the measures that respond to small values flip into the opposite.

No metric without signal quality and artefact rate

Long pauses, motion artefacts, implausible beats – the analysis flags them before any metric is computed and shows the value with and without them. Whether a recording comes from the app or from the browser station: the analysis is the same. And where a file demonstrably did not pass through the chain, the report says so — those are raw values, and the report states it instead of claiming a correction that never happened. Implausible outliers are flagged, not taken over

Unfiltered, long pauses change the RR-based short-term values markedly (Eggensperger & Schwarzwald 2017). So the data is cleaned first and compared second, so that measurement errors appear as deviations less often. Normal day-to-day variation remains.

If signal quality or resting time falls short, it says so

Too few beats, too short, too many artefacts — then it says “insufficient data” and gives no verdict. A tool that always says something is not a tool.

The method

One value says little. Only its trend gives it context.

A single HRV value in a horse says little — there is no robust normal value to measure it against. It only becomes meaningful against the animal's own history.

And why in the familiar stall?

Transport, unfamiliar surroundings, unfamiliar people — each of these shifts exactly the autonomic balance you want to observe. For resting HRV the place of recording is not a side issue but part of the method: the analysis requires at least seven minutes of rest in familiar surroundings — below that it gives no result. After loading and a drive this is hard to achieve; in the familiar stall it is achievable every day.

  • During transport, HRV falls by about a third. RMSSD dropped on road transport from 74 to 45 ms (3.5 h) and from 90 to 59 ms (8 h) – measured on the road. Schmidt et al. 2010, Hormones and Behavior
  • Short recordings vary from day to day. In 30 healthy horses, day-to-day variability of HRV was moderate to high in 10- and 60-minute recordings; recording length and time of day influenced most variables, and second-degree AV blocks the RR-based short-term variables – hence identical conditions, comparison with the horse’s own baseline and long pauses reported separately. Eggensperger & Schwarzwald 2017, Journal of Veterinary Cardiology
Why exactly there: protocol and comparability

Same box, same time, same protocol. Only under familiar conditions are two recordings comparable. At home, repeated over weeks, this builds a trend that shows how this horse’s resting HRV develops after a change in housing, feeding or training — the interpretation stays with you.

More on the measuring station: data path, file, privacy

Your client experiences your practice, not our software

She records via your measuring link on your recommendation, and the reply carries your letterhead. Every recording between two visits becomes a touchpoint of your practice.

Raw and corrected value side by side

In horses, long pauses consistent with second-degree AV block, which are common at rest, and genuine artefacts occur in the same series. A filter cannot tell them apart; it discards both. The station therefore reports the raw value and the one over unremarkable pairs. Which share comes from what is your call, not the software's.

Nothing passes through us

The moment your client opens your measuring link, the file is already encrypted against your key on her device. She sends it by whatever route she chooses. We never see it and store it nowhere — in our assessment, therefore, no data processing agreement is needed between us.

Limits we draw ourselves

What CARLON deliberately does not do.

No diagnosis

The software shows measured values, signal quality, trend and deviation. The medical interpretation remains entirely with you. CARLON Equine Visite is not a medical device within the meaning of Regulation (EU) 2017/745.

No treatment recommendation

Even when a trend looks unambiguous: whether anything is changed appears nowhere on the screen. That is your decision, not a metric's.

Frequently asked

Questions from practice – answered briefly.

Does CARLON replace an examination?

No. CARLON prepares measurements and trends. Whether a clinical examination, further diagnostics or treatment is needed is decided by you alone.

What does the owner need?

A Polar H10 chest belt and an Android smartphone or tablet, or a laptop with Chrome or Edge. On iPhone and iPad, Apple does not permit Bluetooth in the browser; the page directs you to a capable device there.

What happens after cancellation?

The application switches to a reduced mode. Your data stays on the device and can still be opened and exported. We could not delete it — it is with you.

Do I need a data processing agreement?

In our assessment, no. The cockpit runs entirely in your browser, and the owner's recording never reaches us either: her measuring station creates a file on her own device, which she sends to you herself. There is no relay at our end — and therefore nothing a data processing agreement could be about. Please check this for your practice.

How good can a layperson’s recording be?

Every recording carries its own measurement report: share of usable beat pairs, artefact rate, sensor, timing resolution. Too few beats or too short a rest is flagged in the recording instead of showing a number. And the raw data comes with it: every single beat interval.

Does this create a duty to keep watching?

No. CARLON sends no alerts, and the owner sees no classification — it only comes into being in your cockpit, when you open it. A file sent to you is a delivery like an email: when you review it is up to you, communicated to your clients as before. For emergencies, what has always applied still applies: the phone call.

Where does the baseline come from?

From this horse's own resting recordings: three of its own recordings within 30 days form the baseline; from the fourth, the cockpit shows the deviation from it. Until then the cockpit shows the starting value from the file, explicitly not as a baseline, and how many of the horse's own recordings are still missing. It does not pretend to know something it does not.

Where is my data, and how do I back it up?

Only in your browser on your device – no cloud, no server of ours. Under Documentation, “Back up” creates a backup file; “Restore backup” brings everything back on a new device or browser, including your activation. If a recent backup is missing, the cockpit shows it at the top. On the clinic plan the records are also kept in the clinic folder on your network drive.

Can I charge for reviewing a recording?

You decide that under the German fee schedule for veterinarians (GOT) – to our knowledge (November 2026) there is no dedicated GOT item for telemedicine. CARLON delivers measurements, trends and a measurement report; whether this becomes a consultation and how you charge for it is up to you. Reviewing does not replace an examination.
