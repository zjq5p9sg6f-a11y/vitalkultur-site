Summary for supervisors, ethics committees and procurement

CARLON HRV Research is an iPhone app that records beat-to-beat RR intervals from Polar H10 chest straps and Movesense Flash sensors and exports the original data for analysis in Kubios, Python, R or MATLAB. This dossier documents what we checked, how, and with which result — including the errors we found along the way.

For supervisors

  • All 277 metric checks agree with independent references (NeuroKit2, SciPy, Task Force definitions) within predefined tolerances; most to machine precision.
  • Artefact detection follows Lipponen & Tarvainen (2019), the method used in Kubios, and flags the same beats as NeuroKit2 in 18 of 18 series.
  • Raw RR data are exported unmodified; the analysis of record stays in your software.
  • Re-verified on build 1.4 (2026102801): app output byte-identical to the certified run ✓.

For ethics committees

  • No account and no server: recordings stay on the researcher's iPhone; vitalkultur receives no study data.
  • Participants are managed under pseudonymous IDs; exports can replace participant codes with a stable pseudonym (pseudonymised, not anonymised).
  • Every record carries an append-only audit trail (ALCOA+) secured by a SHA-256 hash chain, and every complete export a W3C PROV record of where its data came from.
  • Not a medical device; for research and wellness use.

For procurement

  • One-time purchase on the App Store; no subscription, no per-participant fees.
  • Institutions can buy licences in volume through Apple School Manager or Apple Business Manager (research institutes, clinics) and assign them to devices or users.
  • Tested with Polar H10 and Movesense Flash; iPhone with iOS 17 or later.
  • One iPhone runs up to four sensors at a time and reads them all out; no extra hardware or software needed for recording.

A typical study workflow

1 · RegisterEach strap once, linked to a pseudonymous participant ID.
2 · StartUp to four sensors in one run; they record to their own memory, the phone can stay in the bag.
3 · MarkTap event buttons at phase changes (rest, training, recovery); each event carries its clock time.
4 · Read outAll sensors in one go; each recording lands with its participant and real start time.
5 · ExportOriginal RR as CSV (with Kubios import settings), TXT, FIT, EDF+ or WFDB; event and segment tables; JSON with audit trail and PROV; PDF report and every figure as a publication-ready file.

Segment table: duration, beats, heart rate, RMSSD, SDNN and pNN50 for each phase between two events, computed with the same validated code as in this dossier. For a thesis we recommend reporting the values from your analysis software and using the table as a cross-check.

Methods text for a thesis

RR intervals were recorded with a Polar H10 chest strap using the CARLON HRV Research app (version 1.4; vitalkultur, Germany) and exported without filtering for analysis in Kubios HRV Scientific.

What was checked

Artefact detection. The app classifies beats with the Lipponen & Tarvainen (2019) method and was compared beat by beat with NeuroKit2 signal_fixpeaks, including series with inserted missed, extra and ectopic beats.

Threshold floor. Lipponen & Tarvainen scale their thresholds with local variability; during hard exercise beat-to-beat variability shrinks to a few milliseconds and normal beats are flagged. The app therefore bounds both thresholds below at 30 ms. Chosen by the rule “the largest floor that loses no detection in any artefact class” (candidates and rule were set after a first exploration on an external gym recording — disclosed) in an injection study (8 base series × 6, NeuroKit2 0.2.13 as reference): no loss of sensitivity, false alarms 4.13 → 0.09 per 1000 beats. With the floor set to 0 the app flags exactly the beats NeuroKit2 flags (18 of 18 series); with 30 ms it matches NeuroKit2 carrying the same floor in 18 of 18.

Real ectopic beats (MIT-BIH). On the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (44 recordings, every beat annotated by cardiologists; paced records excluded) the detection finds isolated ectopic beats in otherwise regular rhythm — ventricular 85 %, supraventricular 94 % — but not frequent ectopy: in runs with ≥ 5 % ectopic beats around them only 11 % (V) and 6 % (S). The threshold floor changes none of these numbers. Because HRV assumes sinus rhythm (Task Force 1996), the app adds a rhythm check: the share of beats with a premature pattern; from 5 % it shows a data-quality note. On recording level it flags 17 of 19 recordings outside sinus rhythm (≥ 5 % ectopy or atrial fibrillation) and 1 of 25 others. It is a data-quality note, not a rhythm diagnosis.

Movesense ECG → R peaks. For in-sensor Movesense recordings the app detects R peaks in the 256 Hz ECG itself. Against Pan–Tompkins (1985) on 4 real recordings (434 reference beats) it missed 0 and added 0; beats inside motion-disturbed segments, where the reference itself counts artefacts, were judged on the ECG trace and not scored. A beat whose shape is distorted by motion is kept when it falls on the expected beat (±20 % of the recent median interval).

One number everywhere. App screen, PDF report, CSV and both JSON profiles take every headline metric from the same certified analysis; an automated test reads each of them back and compares.

Metrics. Computed on the same artefact-corrected series and compared against an independent implementation with the same definition and parameters: NeuroKit2 where available, otherwise NumPy/SciPy following the original literature.

Movesense ECG → RR. For Movesense in-sensor recordings the app derives RR intervals from the raw ECG (256 Hz) with its own R-peak detector. Against a Polar H10 worn at the same time, all 166 H10 beats have a Movesense counterpart: bias +0.01 ms, SD 0.30 ms. The Movesense recording started 12 beats earlier and ran longer, which is why the two series have different beat counts.

Test series. Eleven synthetic vectors with known properties (constant, alternating, known SD, single and dual tones, white/pink/brown noise, an oracle difference series, time reversal and permutation) and 7 real recordings, including three Polar H10 straps worn simultaneously, a simultaneous Polar H10 / Movesense recording and a 90-min series (6,822 beats) whose last third has twice the variability.

Conventions and comparability with Kubios

  • pNN50 divides NN50 by n−1 (differences); NeuroKit divides by n. The NN50 count is checked exactly.
  • SD2 uses the Brennan form √(2·SDNN² − ½·SDSD²) as in Kubios.
  • DFA α1 uses scales 4–16 beats (Peng 1995, Kubios), non-overlapping windows.
  • Sample entropy: m = 2, r = 0.2 × SD of the series (as NeuroKit2's default). For recordings longer than 3000 beats it is computed on the most recent 3000 beats (quadratic cost); the reference is computed on the same span — the longest test series has 6,822 beats.
  • Artefact correction (time-preserving): each run of consecutive flagged intervals is replaced by N equal intervals of the same total duration, N = the sum of whole multiples of the local median where every interval of the run is one (±15 %; missed beats, also when classified ectopic), otherwise run length + missed − extra beats as classified (so a fast heart-rate change neither gains nor loses beats); hence a missed beat is split, an extra beat merged, a premature beat with its compensatory pause averaged. Beats classified as normal are never changed. Only a single flagged interval without compensation is replaced by the mean of its neighbours (the one case that changes the duration; 0.48 s in a 48-min and 2.0 s in a 100-min exercise recording, while the time axis of the export stays the original one). Detection is identical to NeuroKit2; the correction step is the app's own and documented per beat in the export (value × count, correction type). All metrics use this series; the export keeps the uncorrected original.
  • Triangular index / TINN use a bin width of 1/128 s anchored at the smallest RR.
  • Spectrum: the whole recording (up to 24 h), natural cubic spline, 4 Hz, Welch with a 256-sample Hann window (64 s), 50 % overlap, Task Force bands integrated exactly at the band limits.
  • Baevsky stress index: SI = AMo / (2 · Mo · MxDMn), with AMo in %, mode Mo and range MxDMn in s; reported as √SI. Bin width 50 ms for humans (as Kubios). From 10 min on, the median over 5-min windows on a fixed grid (300·k, 300·(k+1)] s by interval end (final window only from 240 s); no detrending.
  • Stationarity: augmented Dickey–Fuller test with constant, lag order 12·(n/100)^¼, p-value after MacKinnon (1994); identical to statsmodels adfuller with the same lags (automated test).
Kubios may use other window lengths or detrending. Frequency-domain and non-linear values from the app and from Kubios are only comparable with the same settings — report the values from your analysis software.

Errors found and fixed during validation

A validation is only worth something if it can find errors. These were found and corrected before this dossier was issued:

  1. Frequency bands for humans. LF/HF for human recordings was computed with the frequency bands of a non-human preset of the shared signal-processing engine. Fixed with a dedicated human profile (Task Force 1996), covered by an automated test.
  2. Band integration. Spectral bins on a band edge were counted fully in both neighbouring bands (LF of a real 30-min recording +25 %). Fixed by exact integration at the band limits.
  3. Test expectation. Two older tests required time symmetry of the Lipponen–Tarvainen correction, which is directional (also in NeuroKit2). Replaced by index equality with NeuroKit2.
  4. Spectrum of long recordings. Resampling stopped after 5000 beats or 2048 s: LF/HF of every recording longer than 34 min came from its first 34 min. The reference had copied the same limits and therefore agreed. Fixed in app and reference; a 90-min test series whose last third has twice the variability now fails the old version (LF −44 %).
  5. Artefact flags during exercise. An external comparison (gym recording, 5,544 beats) showed 129 flagged beats against 29 corrections of another program; NeuroKit2 also flags 129. Most were normal beats at high heart rate. Fixed with the validated threshold floor above (129 → 55).
  6. JSON export. The JSON file recomputed its headline metrics itself (SD2 pair variant, sample entropy on the first instead of the last beats). It now carries the certified values; a test compares every field.

Independent external audit (26 Oct 2026)

An external auditor received a blind package — one synthetic recording with injected errors at undisclosed positions and three real recordings — and recomputed every number from the raw RR intervals. It located all injected errors and confirmed hash, audit chain, time axis, time-domain metrics, DFA α1 and segment tables exactly. It also found these defects, all fixed in build 1.4 (2026102801):

  1. Correction cascade. A first time-preserving correction (moving peaks as in NeuroKit2) also changed the normal beat after a premature beat and its pause (427 → 491 ms), which shifted DFA α1 windows. Replaced by the run-based correction above; a test pins the auditor's case (pair averaged, neighbour unchanged).
  2. Stationarity test. The ADF test returned t ≈ 0 for every recording (the matrix inverse was not formed), so every report said “not stationary”. Fixed; now identical to statsmodels.
  3. Provenance. Recordings without stored metadata were labelled live recordings at 1/1024 s. Every export now carries an origin derived from stored metadata and the RR values (live, strap memory, sensor memory, import, synthetic example or unknown), never assumed.
  4. Quality score. Recordings without a stored end time scored as 0 s long, and the metadata score was fixed at 100. Both now follow the data; the scales are documented in the export.
  5. Smaller items. Baevsky windows on a fixed grid; DFA α1 windows with a residual artefact or α1 < 0 marked invalid; window assignment on a millisecond axis; TINN and √SI in CSV and JSON; one confidence label per metric; rhythm-check denominator n − 10.

Disclosed post-hoc choices: the Baevsky tolerance (≤ 1 % rel.) was set after the first run, because the first reference implemented a different formula variant; spectral values below 0.01 are treated as zero (rounding noise for constant or alternating series), LF/HF is then not applicable.

Scope and limits

This dossier verifies the computation: given the same RR series, the app produces the same metrics as independent reference implementations, and it records and exports data in a traceable way. It is not a clinical validation and does not assess the accuracy of the Polar H10 or Movesense sensors themselves; for that, see the published validation literature on these sensors. CARLON HRV Research is not a medical device.

Rhythm. HRV metrics assume sinus rhythm. Artefact correction removes sparse disturbances; frequent ectopy and atrial fibrillation are not corrected (see MIT-BIH above) — the rhythm check flags such recordings, and their HRV values should not be interpreted.

Changes in dossier 1.4

Found in a field recording by a study partner and fixed: at abrupt heart-rate changes the run correction could add beats (in a 100-min exercise recording 9 beats that did not exist, e.g. 533–801 ms counted as 8 instead of 6 intervals); the number of intervals per run is now set by whole multiples of the local rhythm (missed beats) or else by the classification, with tests built from these sequences; a second field recording (contact loss at the end of a gym session, double intervals classified ectopic) showed why both rules are needed · the DFA α1 time course is now certified window by window (2,854 windows in 18 series against Tarvainen 2002 detrending and NeuroKit2 DFA, largest deviation 7e-11) – NeuroKit2's own tarvainen2002 detrending was found faulty in version 0.2.13 (its second-difference matrix is incomplete) and is not used as reference · the 5 % limit for DFA α1 now cites its evidence (Rogers et al. 2021; Schaffarczyk et al. 2022) · λ = 500 stated as the Kubios default · re-verified on build 1.4 (2026102801).
