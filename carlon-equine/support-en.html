Frequently asked questions

Pairing the Polar H10

Moisten the electrode areas of the strap, on a horse, electrode gel is practically essential for a clean signal because of the impedance of the coat. Fit the strap and open the measuring station in your browser, you can connect the H10 there with a single tap (on an Android device, laptop or tablet using Chrome or Edge; not on an iPhone or iPad, and not in Safari or Firefox, which do not support Web Bluetooth). The signal indicator shows you when the contact is good enough. Once the app is in the App Store, you will also be able to connect the strap there under ‘Devices’.

For a detailed guide, including common mistakes, see Polar H10 on the horse: measuring resting HRV correctly.

On-device recording (without an iPhone in range)

The H10 can record for hours directly on the strap, for example overnight in the stable, out at pasture or during transport. This option will come with the iOS app: once it is in the App Store, you will start the recording there, disconnect the iPhone and sync later with a single click (important: the H10 has only one recording slot; an older recording must be deleted before a new one is started). For now, you can measure live using the browser-based measuring station, the device must stay within Bluetooth range while you do so.

Opening the analysis in your browser

Every JSON export opens directly in the browser viewer at carlon-equine, by drag and drop or by selecting the file. When you open and view your own files, no data is transmitted: the viewer runs entirely locally in your browser (zero upload). No login, no installation. The measuring station does not transmit anything to us either: it creates a file on your device, which you pass on yourself, if you have scanned your practice's notice, the file is encrypted (Section 12b of the Terms and Conditions).

Passing the analysis on to your vet

When the measurement is finished, the measuring station creates a file named CARLON_<Horse>_<Date>.json on your device, you send it to your practice by whichever channel you already use (e.g. email or a messaging app). It contains only your horse's measurement; the owner's name and the vet's contact details are not included. If your practice has a CARLON notice, the file is sealed for that practice. The practice opens it in the browser and sees the full analysis. Once the app is in the App Store, you will also be able to share the export from there with a single tap.

Why doesn't CARLON make a diagnosis?

CARLON Equine is a research and observation tool, not a medical device under the MDR or FDA regulations. All notes on the screen are observational notes, not diagnoses; what they mean is for your veterinary practice to assess. That way, the clinical finding is always the vet's to make.

Where do the reference ranges come from?

For the most part, from us, and this is stated wherever such a value appears. There is no generally accepted normal range for HRV in horses; the ranges that CARLON compares against are our own reasoned definitions and are explicitly labelled as such. Where a value really does come from a study, the study is named alongside it. For this reason, the most informative figure is not the absolute value but the deviation from your horse's own earlier measurements. How each stage performs its calculations is set out openly in the methods documentation.

Kubios / your own analysis

The export is Kubios-compatible (raw RR as JSON and as CSV, with a single column, rr_ms) and carries a SHA-256 integrity hash plus a W3C PROV provenance chain. This means you can recalculate every figure independently and demonstrate that the file has not been altered in transit, in R, Python or Kubios.
