Methods documentation

Sensor, artefact-correction algorithm, data flow, analysis gates, sources and limitations of heart rate variability recording in horses.
Version 2026-08-14 · Measurement profile equine-v1 · For institutions, research partners and ethics committees

Purpose of this document

If you take measurements with CARLON Equine and need to describe the results in an application, a study or a horse’s record, you need the numbers behind them. This document states every filter stage with its actual threshold and the source for it. It also states explicitly where a value is a reasoned setting of our own rather than a value from the literature. The values are checked against the shipped code on every test run.

1 · Recording

What is recorded are RR intervals, the time between two heartbeats, in milliseconds. The browser measuring station records no raw ECG signal, no images or audio and no location data; the iOS app can also save an ECG strip (Vet mode) and, if enabled, a GPS track.

2 · Filter chain

Certain rhythm irregularities are normal in the resting horse, and they severely distort the indices if the calculation is unfiltered. The chain runs in a fixed order; each stage is listed below with its actual threshold. All values belong to the measurement profile equine-v1, exactly one profile is supplied; there is no evidence to support the values for other species.

Order in the code: The detection stages run first and the plausibility stamp afterwards. This is deliberate: an interval that has been detected as a physiological block may be longer than the regular upper limit. A plausibility filter applied first would have cut out exactly these pauses before they could be detected as a block at all.

Plausibility stamp

Discard obviously impossible intervals

An interval is considered plausible between 200 ms (equivalent to 300 beats/min) and 8,000 ms (7.5 beats/min). Beyond 12,000 ms, a value is considered unusable even as a block candidate.

The upper limit was deliberately set high: a longest RR interval of more than 4 s is common in healthy, lightly trained horses. A narrower limit would cut out precisely those pauses that are physiological.our own setting There is no published plausibility range for horses.

Stage 1 · T-wave merging

Merge double-counted beats

If a T wave is wrongly detected as a separate beat, the result is two intervals that are too short instead of one correct interval. This is detected using a running median window (300–3,500 ms): if an interval is below 0.5 times the local median and above the absolute minimum of 100 ms, it is merged with the following interval rather than discarded.

Stage 2 · Artefact correction

Method after Lipponen & Tarvainen (2019), adapted for the horse

An established method for detecting ectopic and erroneous beats from the distribution of the interval differences. The threshold factor is α = 5.2; the dispersion is estimated from the quartile deviation and is therefore robust to individual outliers.

Stage 3 · Second-degree AV block · the crucial step

Detect longer pauses and report them separately

Second-degree atrioventricular block is a physiological finding in the resting horse: one beat is dropped and the interval is about twice as long. It disappears during exercise. It is detected from the ratio to the local median (window of 10 beats): if an interval is between 1.8 and 2.2 times the local median and the neighbouring intervals deviate by no more than 25%, it is marked as a presumed physiological pause. Only P waves on the ECG can show whether it really is an AV block. our own setting

Why this matters: if such pauses are included uncorrected in the calculation, RMSSD and SDNN rise considerably, in our test series (300 beats, three pauses, as tested on 13 November 2026) raw RMSSD was 285 ms, compared with 86 ms after the pauses were excluded. As a result, the very markers that respond to low values swing to the opposite extreme.

Exactly what happens to the block: It does not count towards the artefact rate and so does not count against signal quality (it appears in neither the numerator nor the denominator). In the corrected series it is nevertheless interpolated like any other marked interval; it is retained as a separate label alongside the series. If you want to analyse the pauses yourself, you will therefore find them in the dataset, they are simply not included in the time-domain indices.

Stage 4 · Interpolation

Interpolation of marked intervals

Every interval that one of the stages above has marked as not regular is corrected by cubic spline interpolation (Catmull-Rom) through the nearest regular neighbouring beats. In other words, it is estimated rather than deleted, the original marking is retained alongside the series.

Accuracy over completeness

The measurement profile includes four sensitivity stages modelled on Kubios (0.4 / 0.6 / 0.9 / 1.2 s, scaled by a factor of 3 relative to human values). They are recorded in the dataset as profile information, but do not control the correction path in the current version: every marked beat is corrected, regardless of the size of the deviation. We state this here because a methods section that presents a threshold as applied when it is not would be demonstrably wrong at precisely this point.

4 · Analysis gates

Not every recording supports every analysis. Where there is too little data to go on, no result is shown, not even a cautious estimate. A systematically biased figure does more harm than no figure at all.

Breathing pattern profile: where the comparison limits come from

The reference study is Nyerges-Bohák et al. (2025), Equine Veterinary Journal, DOI 10.1111/evj.14414. It compares n = 40 horses: 20 with severe equine asthma (sEA), BAL-referenced, against 20 controls with no respiratory abnormalities, no BAL was performed in the controls. The study reports group differences and names SD2, SDNN, TINN and SD2/SD1 as the more discriminating measures. It does not contain a diagnostic threshold.

Our thresholds lie between the published group means, not exactly midway. They are therefore our own reasoned derivation, not values taken from the literature.set by us Core thresholds: SDNN 55 ms, SD2 65 ms, SD2/SD1 1.50, TINN 300 ms, DFA-α₁ 0.70, sample entropy 0.80.

We have removed one threshold without replacing it

Until August 2026, an RMSSD value of 95 ms was also listed here. It was attributed to the reference study, but appears there only as a 95% confidence interval — and it was above the mean of the healthy control group in the same study (86.0 ms). A threshold that would have flagged the majority of the healthy study horses could be refuted from the very paper it cited. It was removed and not replaced, group means are not thresholds.

Limits of this classification

This is a classification of measured values, not a diagnosis. The profile is no substitute for an examination; for airway diagnosis, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) remains the gold standard. Any assessment is a matter for the veterinary practice alone.

5 · Data flow

The path the data takes is part of the method, because it determines who has control over what.

  1. The strap transmits the RR intervals via Bluetooth to the device of the person taking the measurement (browser or app).
  2. The recording is created and stays on that device. Nothing is stored with us along the way, and nothing is uploaded.
  3. To share a recording, a file is created, which the person taking the measurement sends themselves, by whatever channel they already use. If the measurement was opened via a practice's link, the file is already encrypted for that practice.
  4. The receiving organisation imports the file into its own system. The complete dataset is also included as an open FHIR R5 bundle.

For a data protection impact assessment, this means that no service of ours sits between the person taking the measurement and the receiving organisation. The recording party decides which personal data a recording carries, the file itself contains no contact details.

6 · Traceability

Every reported index carries a provenance note stating whether it was calculated by the recording app and passed through the artefact-correction algorithm, or derived here from the raw series. If the note is missing, we do not claim that the artefact-correction algorithm was applied — the report then states explicitly that the values are unfiltered and must not be compared with reference values.

This is not a formality. A document that reports an artefact correction that never ran makes raw values look as though they have been checked, and in the resting horse, raw and corrected values are far apart (see stage 3: an RMSSD of 285 ms rather than 86 ms). Hence the rule: Artefact correction is mentioned only where it has actually taken place.

In its own section, ‘Method & limits’, the report sets out the chain that was actually run, together with its sources. Raw data (RR series) can be exported at any time as JSON, CSV or in a Kubios-compatible format, so that every calculation can be verified independently.

7 · Limits

  • Not a medical device. CARLON Equine is not intended to detect, monitor, treat or alleviate disease. No diagnosis, no treatment recommendations. Regulation (EU) 2017/745 applies to devices for human use and does not apply to horses. In advertising, the German Heilmittelwerbegesetz (Act on Advertising in the Field of Healthcare) applies to animals as well; CARLON therefore makes no promise to detect or remotely assess disease.
  • Not an emergency channel. Sending a file is not a phone call. If there is an acute problem, the rule is the same as always: seek veterinary attention immediately.
  • No reliable reference value. There is no universally valid reference range for HRV in the horse. What is informative is comparison with the animal's own history, not the absolute value. One example shows how thin the evidence is: the healthy control horses in the reference study cited above had a mean RMSSD of 86.0 ms, below our own working range (set by us, not a norm). This does not refute our range (different recording duration, different population, n = 20), but it is the first hard data point against it. We list it here rather than leaving it out.
  • Movement limits what can be concluded. The indices require a calm recording that is as stationary as possible. Movement artefacts are corrected, but every correction is an estimate.
  • One profile, one species. The values above apply to the horse. For other species they are unsubstantiated; the measurement profile permits other values but does not recommend any.
  • Disputed indices remain disputed. The LF/HF ratio is traditionally interpreted as sympathovagal balance; this reading is scientifically contested. We report the value as a trend against the baseline, not as an absolute statement.

8 · Sources

Grouped by what a paper actually supports in our method. A paper that fits the topic but does not contain the cited statement is not listed here as evidence, it would do more harm than a missing source.

What we withdrew in August 2026

When we checked every citation against the primary papers, several attributions could not be confirmed. Those affected included: the origin of the RMSSD and SDNN bands (Stucke 2015, von Borell 2007, Ille 2014, none of these papers supports them), a resting heart rate range, an artefact limit and a reliability figure whose purported sources could not be traced. One reference, as it appeared in our version, had an entirely invented title, journal and page number. The affected figures have either been removed or now appear, wherever they occur, as our own specification, none has been attributed to a substitute source.
